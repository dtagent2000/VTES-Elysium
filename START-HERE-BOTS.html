ELYSIUM

Trainbots — practice opponents for your table

Bot v0.3.10  ·  launcher v1.0.0  ·  zero dependencies  ·  just Node

What is this

Trainbots are headless practice opponents that take real seats at your online table.

They join a room exactly like a human client does, upload a sample weenie-bleed deck, follow the turn order, influence vampires, announce bleeds on the table's own machine-readable lines, answer the resolvers, play stealth against block attempts, and take damage when a bleed lands on them. Humans and bots mix freely — any combination up to a full five-seat table. The bot follows the core rules: crypt and card uniqueness, one same-named modifier per action, one action per minion per turn, one discard per turn, crypt fetches at the rulebook price (4 transfers + 1 pool), and it takes the Edge when a bleed goes through. Card plays and bleeds animate on everyone's table, and with the Played-tab resolvers on, a spoken Block! makes the bot wait for the referendum's verdict line instead of assuming the block lands.

Files the bots need, side by side: elysium-bot.js and elysium-cardfx.json (the card-knowledge library). On start, look for the cardfx loaded: 2364 lib… line — a ⚠ cardfx NOT loaded warning means the bot is running blind: plain bleeds only, no card plays, no stealth. The launcher and the arena need elysium-bot-table.js / elysium-bot-arena.js in the same folder too.

Pacing: at a human table the bots think in beats — announcements ~2 s apart and reactions ~1.6 s (defaults; tune with --pace-ms). Test suites run them at machine speed.

The Reaction timer: five seconds into an unanswered Block? the bot rings the table's Reaction timer — the same ⏱ stopwatch you trigger yourself, set per room (3–30 s, default 5). The countdown now shows who called it right above the number, and the bot still waits its own full, patient window (default 25 s, --ask-secs) before moving on: the stopwatch is the nudge, not the deadline. Verdicts pulse too: Block succeeds/fails and Vote passes/fails pop as say-style bubbles for the whole table, and a misclicked Block is taken back out loud with the resolver's × — the bot reopens its window as if nothing happened.

The host referee kit reaches the bots: right-click any opponent's pool — the mat, the Table panel row, or the L4 globe itself — for ash heap, Set pool… / Pool ±1/−2 (Kine Resources Contested and friends), oust/return and pass-their-turn. Bots apply it, acknowledge in chat, and a bot drained to 0 is ousted by it.

Three personalities ship out of the box: Novice, Grinder and Shark — same facts, different judgement.

Quick start — one bot
  1. Start the server as usual (start-elysium) and open the Elysium client.
  2. Create an online room (lobby on), pick your seat and import your deck.
  3. In a terminal, from the Elysium folder:
    node elysium-bot.js --server ws://127.0.0.1:8123 --room ROOMNAME --pass PASSWORD
  4. The bot picks a free seat, uploads its playbook and greets the table.
  5. Press Start in the client. Play.

Optional flags: --persona novice|grinder|shark, --name Alice, --seat N, --ask-secs 25, --deck playbook.json, --cardfx elysium-cardfx.json.

A whole table of them

Create the room and take your seat first, then seat several bots in one command:

node elysium-bot-table.js --room ROOMNAME --pass PASSWORD --count 3

Shark, Grinder and Novice fill the free seats (staggered arrivals, one process, Ctrl+C removes them all). Rotate or repeat with --personas shark,shark,novice, rename with --names A,B,C, and give them real decks with --decks nosferatu,tremere,malkavian (the registered archetypes: hacking, sewer, malkavian, tremere, nosferatu — table.js v1.1.0).

Novice — aggression 0.8 · insight 0.1 Barely reads the table. Spends stealth into obvious intercept. Forgiving to play against.
Grinder — aggression 1.0 · insight 0.5 The default. Solid, unremarkable, respects what it has clearly seen.
Shark — aggression 1.2 · insight 0.9 Reads shown cards hard: if you've flashed intercept, it saves its stealth and takes the fight elsewhere.

The read is honest inference — hands never travel the wire, so the bot only knows what you have shown: played cards, disciplines on the board, hand counts.

The table the bot likes

Bots play best on: Structured board · Tournament mode OFF · the three resolvers ON (Block / Vote / Combat in the Played peek). That gives them the live referendum and the resolve lines — a Block verdict can answer the bot's own "Block?" without anyone typing.

A Classic table still works: the bot follows the bleed announces and the say phrases, pitches a tidy camp at its own table edge, and says one polite line about its preferred setup — then plays along. No hard locks, ever: the table referees.

Talking to the bot
  • Its native language is the table's own: the Bleed 1/2/3/X… submenu, +X bleed…, the say phrases (Hold on…, Pass, Block!, It resolves) and the resolver verdict lines. Use them and the bot keeps up by itself.
  • Chat grammar (blunt overrides): bleed N — the bot takes N pool now · vote N — the bot loses N pool to a referendum · block / no — answer its question the simple way (block always goes straight to combat).
  • If a bleed at the bot is never resolved, it discards it at the turn wrap with a polite note — it never applies stale damage on its own.
Humans + bots together

Yes — by design. Bots are ordinary clients: same room, same seats, any mix. Friends can join over your Cloudflare tunnel as usual (https://…), while bots connect with plain ws:// next to the server (same machine or LAN) — the tunnel terminates TLS before the server anyway, so that is the natural place for them.

Rule of thumb: humans arrive through the front door, bots live in the cellar.

Watch them fight — the arena

Let the personas settle it among themselves, with statistics:

node elysium-bot-arena.js --matches 10 --bots 3 --start-pool 15

Headless matches to last-standing, seat rotation for fairness, and a per-persona summary (wins, damage dealt/taken, decisions). Add --trace DIR for JSONL traces of every decision the bots make — the raw material for tuning them.

Small samples lie: seat 1 starts with fewer transfers, so run enough matches before crowning a persona.

Not yet

The bot does not block as a defender or fight real combat (that is milestone M2 — the duel view is where it will happen), plays no master cards or attachments (v0.4), and joins votes only through the chat grammar. It reads the combat/vote tallies but never writes them.

Live-test checklist — this stage
  1. Solo smoke (Structured, resolvers on): seat, greeting, turn flow, influence, bleed asks; block → one-round combat, no → through.
  2. Machine lines: the log shows "NAME bleeds for N." from the bot; card plays read "plays Computer Hacking — bleeds … for 2".
  3. Bleed the bot with the Bleed submenu → it says Hold on… then Pass; say It resolves → it applies and acknowledges the exact amount.
  4. +X bleed… after your announce → the bot re-passes; resolves at the total.
  5. Block resolver on the bot's bleed → the verdict line alone answers its question.
  6. Say Block! against its action → it plays stealth (if usable); post "block fails" + Pass → the bleed goes through.
  7. Personas: show the table a wake/intercept card, then block-attempt a Shark vs a Novice — Shark saves its stealth, Novice spends it.
  8. Classic table: one polite tip; its globe and cards camp at its own table edge; bleed lines still tracked.
  9. Full table: elysium-bot-table.js --count 3 — bots bleed each other and the loop closes without you (watch the chat).
  10. Deflection: re-announce the bleed with a pool target naming another player — the bot un-arms and the named target's bot arms.
  11. Rough edges: kill a bot process mid-game (table continues), wrong password (clean refusal), bleed a bot to 0 (bounty routes to its predator).
  12. Rate behaviour: a long, fast multi-bot game never drops a bot (the send queue self-paces under the server's limit).
