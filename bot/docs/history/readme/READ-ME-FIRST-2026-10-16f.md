## 1. The dodge (Johan, 16 Oct 17:47 + 18:06; question A, 21:20 + 21:27)
- **What it was:** `decide('combat-strike')` dodged a known strike only when it would empty or torpor the vampire or was aggravated, and an unknown one only at blood ≤ 1.
- **Now:** a dodge answers what the opposing strike does to me (damage + steal; their crows land anyway) against what MY strike would deal this round.
  - My number is `_strikePick(vamp, theirDodge)`: the steal, the strike card or weapon, or the fist (1 + strength + the crypt modifier; 0 at long range). It is cut out of `_resolveCombatRound`'s strike choice and that site plays its pick, so the dodge is weighed against the strike that would really be struck. No side effects.
  - **One dodge in hand:** a known strike of 2 or more that is more than mine → dodge (`known-out`).
  - **Two or more** (`_dodgeCopies`; Johan: "man kan offra en Dodge om man har två på handen även vid 1 skada"): a known strike of 1 or more that does at least as much as mine → dodge (`known-two`). When mine does more — Roundhouse for 3 against their 1 — I strike (`known-keep`; Johan: "Roundhouse först").
  - Aggravated (`known-aggr`) and the survival rule (`known-survival`) as before.
  - **The finisher exception** (`known-finisher`, `unknown-finisher`): when MY strike finishes their vampire and theirs does not finish mine (survival comes first), I strike and keep the dodge. With two dodges: a strike that takes them to 0 blood or into torpor (Johan's words). With one: into torpor only — emptying a vampire that hunts back next turn is not worth the 2 or 3 blood the dodge would have saved me (my recommendation, which Johan took for now). Their blood is `_stealAvail()` (unknown → no finisher). My damage that lands either way (Carrion Crows, a borne Murder of Crows) does not count: the strike has to make the difference.
  - **A dodge their crows make useless is kept** (`known-wasted`): when their environmental damage alone would put me into torpor, the dodge saves nothing, so I strike.
  - An unknown strike (I strike first) is read on the expected strike Majesty already uses (`_expOppStrike`, shared with `_ceCtx`): `unknown-out` / `unknown-two` / `unknown-finisher` on the same rule. With `combatEndStats` 0 there is no expected number, and the old blood ≤ 1 stands.
- **A note for the meta layer** (Johan, 21:27): what emptying or torporing a vampire is worth depends on the table position — which seat it weakens, who profits, where my prey stands — not only on this exchange. The finisher exception weighs the exchange alone; the meta layer may weigh it later.
- Knob `dodgeOut` (default 1; 0 = v0.6.204's rule byte for byte), in the manifest's knob list.
- Not built: point 4, the knowledge layer's "a deck that never hits for more than 1: dodge at 1 with one dodge too".

## 2. F21 — Revelations against a bot
- **What it was:** only `… Revelations [aus]. Target: <bot>'s pool.` armed the [aus] mode. A vampire aim, no aim, or a bare `plays Revelations.` armed nothing, and the human's pick after the reveal was dropped.
- **Now:** Revelations is prey-locked by its text, so it arms when the actor's prey is this bot, whatever the line's suffix says. A bare play line is [aus].
- After the reveal to a HUMAN actor the bot says how to pick: `NAME, pick the card I discard (Revelations): ⌖ target it in the hand I revealed to you, or type "discard CARD".`
- The pick is read from that actor's targeting `logTo` (client v2.6.159+) or a typed `discard CARD` (any case or punctuation; bare, or addressed `@Name discard CARD` as the bleed grammar is), once, until the turn ends (`_revPick`). The card goes to the ash heap and the bot draws (`_revDiscard`, one body with a bot actor's `Revelations: discard X.`).
- Not a wait: no clock, no rung. The human's own action goes on.

## Things I am not sure about
- **The finisher exception weighs the exchange alone.** Johan's meta-layer note (§1): emptying or torporing a vampire is worth more or less depending on the seat and the table. It reads their blood from the table (`_stealAvail()`); a count it cannot see reads as unknown, and then the exception does not fire. With one dodge it waits for torpor — my recommendation, which Johan took for now.
- **The dodge rule barely shows at these tables.** Most strikes in these decks are for 1, so most dodges are still the old survival rule: the final dodge smoke played 16 (15 survival, 1 `unknown-out`) and kept the dodge twice by the finisher exception. `known-two` and `unknown-two` did not come up — two dodges in hand against a strike that finishes nobody. The unknown case leans on the table model's expected strike (1 + 2 × the seat's combat share, prior-blended), which read 1.5–2.8 for these decks: a seat that has shown a strike for 2 tips it over the floor fast. Not benched; `dodgeOut` is there for the larger re-measure.
- **The Haven's look ahead approximates the minion phase.** It asks each rusher as if it acted now; the real phase orders its actors (the worst blocker first, keep-a-blocker, the probe order), and an earlier actor can change the picture, so a Haven can still sit unused for a turn now and then. Live, the smoke on the PR's first head ran it once: held twice, then played on the predator's Chelsea Blake and rushed through four times (two by the Nosferatu, two by the Tremere); the final smoke held it four times and never played it — two smokes, no measurement.
- **Guardian Angel prevents normal damage only**, as the equipment prevent does: an all-aggravated strike is not prevented by it. Rare at these tables; one line if it matters.
- **The free-rise fix changes three decks' play more than its size suggests.** Before it, a Tremere, Malkavian or Ventrue bot whose stock action (superior Govern, Enchant Kindred) filled a vampire exactly stopped influencing for the rest of the game — four times in the seed-4003 smoke on the PR's first head, three times in the final one and once in the dodge smoke (three matches each). Older numbers for those decks (series, benches, the balanced baselines) may carry that stall; I have not measured how often it happened in them.
- **A human's Haven Uncovered is not read**, and a human's rush through a Haven on a bot's vampire makes the bot lock that vampire (the generic rush-by-chat path). The guide says so; a fix needs the client's Haven line or a rule on the chat path.
- **The Revelations pick has no clock.** If the human never picks, nothing is discarded and the bot draws nothing; the pick closes at the end of the turn. I think that is the right reading of a sandbox table, but it is a choice.
- **The probe reservation (§6)** has been dead since v0.6.143. I did not fix it, because v0.6.143's measurements were made with it dead; Johan wants it measured before and after (next round).
- **The late bounce (§6)** is not fixed and its cause is not pinned: what I know is what the traces show (two looks at one window, the second after the actor's `through`).
- **Main's finding 1 (§8)** is untouched: the verdict line still waits the chat beat after the question.
