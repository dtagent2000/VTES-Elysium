# The round after R4 — the late bounce, the probe reservation, the chat beat (17 Oct 2026)

Agreed with Johan on 16 Oct (21:27, his answers to R4's open points): measure the probe reservation **before and after**;
analyse the chat beat **properly**; and the late bounce found in R4's final smoke. Bot v0.6.205 (PR #25) under it.
Line numbers are v0.6.206's `elysium-bot.js`.

## 1. The late bounce — found and fixed (in v0.6.206)

**What R4's smoke showed:** a bot target bounced a bot's bleed after the actor had resolved it (seed 4003, match 3:
Lloyd Brooks' bleed, the Ventrue's Deflection 0.55 s after the actor's `through`). Twice in ~470 bleeds through across
R4's seven smokes.

**The cause** (both cases came right after a Magic of the Smith):
- The fetch's note read `fetches Bowl of Convergence from the library and equips Aisling Sturbridge (…).`
- That line matches `L12.uEquip` (`^(.+?) (equips|employs) (.+?)\.$`). The actor's prey and predator opened a window
  for an equip that never happened, decided "no block" and said **Pass**.
- The actor's next action was a bleed. Its `Block?` ask was open, but the bleed's announce was still in its send queue
  (the token bucket holds a busy turn's lines for about a second). The phantom Pass reached it there and was taken as
  the prey's answer — before the prey had seen the bleed.
- From then on every answer fell one window late. The prey's real first Pass answered the bleed-total window; the actor
  resolved; the prey's real answer to the total — a Deflection — came after the resolution. The actor's resolution line
  even named the wrong seat ("The bleed goes through: Viktor") while the third seat took the bleed.
- Reproduced on three bots wired at the socket, through each bot's own send queue (the arena's timing): on v0.6.205 the
  bleed resolves unbounced and names Viktor; on v0.6.206 it resolves bounced and names Nora (section 150d).

**The fix — three layers, each enough alone** (checked on scratch copies with one layer each):
1. The note says it otherwise: `Bowl of Convergence comes from the library onto Aisling Sturbridge (…).` It was the only
   bot line that matched an action grammar (all `note()` / `log` texts with *equips* / *employs* checked).
2. `_parseUndirected`: a **bot's** line that reads like an announce but names no minion on that bot's public board opens
   no window. Humans' lines are untouched (their names come from the client).
3. `_askSeen` / `_staleAnswer`: a **bot's** answer that reaches me while the line that opened my window is still in my
   send queue is an answer to an earlier window — ignored, with a `stale-answer` trace row. The opening line is
   `_openLine`: the last action line I queued (the announce, a modifier's add line, the bleed total), never a note. A bot
   answers on that line, so it cannot have seen an action that has not left my queue. A human's answer is never
   second-guessed (the fx clone can show the action before my line).

**Tests:** section 150 (10): the whole turn (a window for the fetch only), the parser guard (and its scope), the stale
answer (and its scope), the anchor (150e, below), and the end-to-end case on the bots' own send queues. On v0.6.205:
5/5 — the five green ones pin the scope.

### 1b. Found by the release's own smoke — two faults in the actor's reactions-only windows (fixed)
**(a) Layer 3's first cut anchored on the wrong line.** It waited for *everything* I had queued before my question. After a
modifier's add line, `_playBleedModifier` pushes my board; my prey answered the add line while the board was still queued,
and its Pass was taken for stale. In the seven matches of the probe series: 19 `stale-answer` rows, every one in the window
after a bleed modifier and every one followed by the window running out its clock (3.3 s): 17 Passes, and one Block! from a
bounce's new target — seed 9701, match 1: Sully bleeds the Ventrue for 1 + Bonding, Deflection sends it to the Nosferatu,
Ryan blocks through Guard Dogs 45 ms later, the actor ignores the attempt, and the Nosferatu takes 2 pool. Fixed:
`_askSeen` anchors on `_openLine`. 150e pins it (red on the first cut, green on v0.6.205, which had no guard).

**(b) An older race: a sleep between a line and its window.** Since v0.6.144's bleed total, the actor slept 150 ms after the
`+0` line — and after a modifier's add line — before it opened the reactions-only window (`_askBlock`). A bot answers that
line in ~50 ms at pace 0; an answer in the gap found no ask open and was lost:
- the window ran out its clock. Counted on R4's smokes (v0.6.205 and its cuts) with `ana-windows.py`: 245 of 379
  bleed-total windows and 14 of 97 modifier windows resolved after 3.3 s with no block attempt (first counted as 253 and
  22; v0.6.207's tool splits out the block contests that failed at the clock);
- a bounce's new target's Block! was lost the same way, so the bleed landed on the blocker (the smoke's one case on the
  anchored bytes: seed 9702, the Ventrue's Deflection sends a 3 to the Nosferatu, The Dowager blocks, the bleed lands).
Fixed: both windows open in the same tick as their line (the 150 ms after the window stays). Section 152 (2): a bounce's
new target at 1 pool blocks at once and keeps its pool (on v0.6.205 the bleed ousts it); a Pass on the total resolves
the bleed in ~0.2 s, not at the 2 s clock.

**Where both bit:** pace 0. At a human table a bot answers after its reaction pace (seconds) and a human cannot answer
within 150 ms. So players never met either; the arena did — every series since v0.6.144 carried ~3 s of dead clock per
bleed total, and the odd lost block attempt.

## 2. The probe reservation — the knob and the measurement

**What it is** (v0.6.143, Johan's stealth-bleed doctrine of 20 Sep): with a heavy bleed in hand, at most one stealth card
and a prey body up, the small bleeders act first and the puncher last; the **reservation** keeps the probers' hands off
the heavy card so the puncher has it. Since v0.6.143 the minion phase set `this._probe` and reset it two lines later, so
the order worked and the reservation never did: the first actor simply played the Govern.

**Built (v0.6.206), default unchanged:** `_probeOrder(actors)` owns the order and the reservation. Knob `probeHold`:
- **0** = as it played since v0.6.143 (the order, no reservation) — the default;
- **1** = the reservation as designed;
- **2** = the reservation only when my actors out-number my prey's unlocked bodies — Johan's read: *with more minions than
  the opponent, 1-bleeds that threaten a Conditioning draw the blocks, and the Govern goes last with the best chance of an
  unblocked action.*

Trace rows: `probe-order` (now with `hold`, `outnumber`, `reserved`) and `probe-hold` (the reservation taking the card
from a prober). Section 151 (5) runs the whole minion phase: at 0 the first actor plays the Govern for 3 and the puncher
bleeds for 1; at 1 the prober bleeds for 1 and the puncher plays the Govern for 3; at 2, the same with one prey body up,
and as at 0 with two.

**The measurement.**
- **In the arena** the order fires about once a match on the V5 table (the seven matches of the probe series, seeds
  9701–9703: 8 times). That proves the knob fires — at 1 every order was reserved (3 of 3), at 2 the two out-numbering
  orders were (2 of 2), with `probe-hold` rows — but it cannot weigh it. The series also ran on the first cut's bytes
  (§1b), so it was stopped at seven matches.
- **In the lab** (`lab-probe.js`, 360 random tables, seeds 1–3; the probe order fired on 207): the same table, one whole
  minion phase per arm, real bots defending. A Malkavian actor with 2–3 vampires, a Govern or a Conditioning in hand and
  at most one stealth card; a prey of 1–3 unlocked bodies from the four V5 decks; a third seat for what is bounced.

| Pool landed on my prey per probe turn | Arm 0 (as played) | Arm 1 (as designed) | Arm 2 (out-numbering) | Paired, arm 2 − arm 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 207 tables | 0.85 | 1.20 | 1.20 | +0.35 ± 0.07 (better 43, worse 5) |
| I out-number its unlocked bodies (91) | 1.92 | 2.73 | 2.73 | +0.80 ± 0.14 (better 43, worse 5) |
| I do not (116) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0 — every bleed blocked in every arm |
| Its heavy bleed: through / blocked (207) | 8 / 199 | 56 / 149 | 56 / 150 | |

- **Johan's read holds exactly.** The reservation pays when I out-number my prey's unlocked bodies: the probers draw the
  blocks and the Govern lands (8 → 56 times). When I do not, every bleed is blocked whatever the order, and arm 1 holds
  the card for nothing (1.13 holds a turn); arm 2 does not. Arm 2 = arm 1 on every table where it holds (checked on 120
  tables where arm 2 was run: the same acts on all 120).
- **The five worse tables** are all a prey with one unlocked body and a wake in hand: it blocks the first prober with the
  body and wakes for the punch. Played the old way, the Govern goes first into the body, and a later bleed of 1 (often
  pumped) gets through. A prey with the cards to stop two actions stops the one that matters, wherever it comes.
- **Blocks per turn are the same** (2.08 / 2.09 / 2.09): the reservation does not buy more combats.
- **By prey deck** (arm 2 − arm 0, all fired tables): Malkavian +0.54, Tremere +0.38, Ventrue +0.34, Nosferatu +0.13
  (its intercept stops most punches anyway).
- **What the lab cannot see:** the turn after (the Govern held is the Govern not spent — in arm 0 it is spent blocked 199
  times in 207), the combats' consequences, the human defender, the other stealth-bleed decks.
- **Size over a match:** the order fires about once a match, and in the arena it out-numbered 17 times in 19 (the probe
  series and the round's three smokes; the lab's random tables: 4 in 9). So under one pool a match for a stealth-bleed
  deck — far below what a 48-match bench can see; the decision is the lab's.

**Proposal for Johan:** `probeHold` 2 as the default (your bait read; it holds only where it pays). Either now, in the
next release (one default value), or with the larger re-measure's bench. The knob stays: 0 is as v0.6.143–206 played.

**Johan's decision (17 Oct, after PR #26 = v0.6.206 was merged): 2 is the default, built in v0.6.207.** `--knob probeHold=0`
is the rollback.
Section 151e plays the default through the minion phase.

**Johan on the five worse tables (17 Oct) — a knowledge-layer note, not built:** counting the prey's wakes as blockers
belongs to the knowledge layer, in two parts: (a) does the deck hold wakes at all (the deck read — its clan, the
disciplines on its vampires, the cards it has shown, the TWD and starter references; CLAUDE.md 64–65), and (b) how likely
it is to hold one in hand NOW, given how many cards it has cycled and how many wakes it has played so far (the
hypergeometric line of 64d / 65d). Then 'out-number' would read my actors against its unlocked bodies plus its expected
wakes.

## 3. The chat beat after a question (main's finding 1, REPLY v0.6.204 §4.1)

**What waits today.** `_drain` (v0.6.100b): after a **chat** line reaches the wire, the next message — whatever it is —
waits `chatPaceMs ?? round(paceMs × 1.6)` = 4.96 s at the client's 3.1 s pace. A question is a chat line. So everything
the bot queues within ~5 s of a question waits with it: the verdict line, the counter (tally) writes, the board, the quick
phrases (*It resolves*, *Pass*), the hold and resume lines, and the "declared from the table controls" line.

**Measured** (the bot's own `_drain` with a recording socket at `paceMs` 3100; the proposal as a scratch patch):

| Scenario | Today | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| E — the verdict on the 3 s clock | 4.96 s | 3.04 s |
| B — the table button decides at 0.08 s | 4.96 s | 0.20 s |
| S — a typed `+1` booked at 0.06 s (the counter) | 4.97 s | 0.20 s |
| H — Hold on at 1.6 s, Pass at 6.0 s: the hold line / the resume line / the verdict | 4.96 / 9.93 / 14.89 s | 1.60 / 6.01 / 6.21 s |
| C — two instruction lines back to back | 4.96 s | 4.96 s (unchanged) |

Today's column is main's measurement to the hundredth (4.96 s; the verdict 8.9 s after the Pass in their hold run).

**Why the beat exists.** v0.6.100 (Johan, 8 Aug: *"chatten respekterar inte pausreglerna"*): bot chat bubbles landed on
top of each other. Two layers came out of it:
- `_pace` — the bot's next **act** waits out the latest bubble;
- `_drain` — the next **message** waits the beat after a chat.

The second layer is the one that also catches status lines. Johan's rule is about acts and bubbles; a log line, a counter
write or a board push is neither. (The client's verdict line pops a small "Block succeeds!" bubble of its own, and the
client shows three bubbles side by side — `sayBubble`'s eviction queue — so a status line beside the question's bubble
stays readable.)

**Options:**
- **A — the beat separates two chat bubbles, nothing else** (main's first idea). A chat waits the beat since the last
  *chat* on the wire; every other message rides the 200 ms cascade since the last send. Plus one exception: a hold or
  resume line skips the beat. It answers the human's own Hold on — they are looking — and v0.6.204 already puts it at the
  head of the queue for that reason.
  - The bot's acts still wait out the bubble in `_pace`, so Johan's v0.6.100 rule stays whole.
  - The order of the lines is unchanged (one queue, first in, first out), so the other bots read the same sequence.
- **B — keep the drain, delay the panel** (main's second idea): `status()` shows the next wait only once everything
  queued before it is on the wire. This fixes the panel order and nothing else: the verdict, the booking and the hold
  lines stay ~5 s late.
- **C — a shorter beat after a question.** It is arbitrary, and still delays status.
- **D — a second, fast queue for status lines.** No: a verdict could overtake the question before it, and the opposing
  bots' parsers assume the order.

**Recommendation: A.**
- **The arena is untouched:** at `paceMs` 0 this whole block is skipped.
- **The suite's pins hold:** 78d — fx→chat on the cascade, chat→chat on the beat.
- **Main's v2.6.162 LIVE tests stay green:** the verdict line comes "no earlier than 2.8 s after the question" — it comes
  at 3.04 s.
- **The panel order:** the next wait shows ~0.2 s before the verdict line, instead of 1.9–4.8 s before it. B is not
  needed unless their rig still shows it.
- **One detail:** the stamp must be the last chat **on the wire**. `say()` stamps `_lastChatAt` when it queues, for
  `_pace`, and a chat after a quick phrase rides the cascade today.

**Size:** one block in `_drain` and a stamp, plus a section (the five scenarios above as assertions; 78d unchanged). No
protocol change, no handoff need beyond a note.

**For Johan:** "kör" on A — then it is built in the next round, with main told in the handoff.

**Johan said go (17 Oct, after the merge): A is built in v0.6.207**, with `_lastChatWireAt` as the detail above says (only a
chat on the wire stamps it; `say()` keeps stamping `_lastChatAt` for `_pace`). Section 153 pins it on the bot's own drain
