64. **THE KNOWLEDGE LAYER — Johan's concept (21 Sep); analysis only, nothing built.**
    - **64a. Johan on the table:** malkavian is acknowledged as the strongest of the original starters (it has won tournaments out of the box) — expect it to pull AWAY as the logic sharpens, and perhaps come down again once a meta layer exists. It must play as well as it can: do not hold it back. A strong malkavian suits the purpose (humans slotting in 1–2 bots to fill seats → stimulating games).
    - **64b. Johan on splitting the pump:** good, above all when a bounce is expected. FOR: a bounce costs less. AGAINST: stealth may be needed on two actions instead of one, and one gives up the chance of a full stack (Govern + Conditioning) twice over with both vampires — explosive bleed traded for a safer advance. Both are right at times; it is a comparison of ODDS — about my own deck (stealth, Governs, Conditionings left in library + hand) AND the opponent's.
    - **64c. THE CONCEPT:** today the odds about MY deck are exact (the composition is known) but about the OPPONENT they are REACTIVE — read from what the table model has seen. A KNOWLEDGE layer makes them PROACTIVE: with a reference (TWD lists, perhaps the starters too) 'Ventrue decks with these vampires run 7 Deflections on average; 4 are played; X cards remain in library + hand → P(one in hand) ≈ XX % → split / do not split'. Until TWD exists: a small bank from the DISCIPLINES on the table and from the bot decks we have — Dominate and Auspex carry bounce, so the expectation exists from turn 1.
    - **64d. CHECKED, it can be derived from what we hold:** in the whole cardfx database the bounce category sits in exactly two disciplines — dom 3 cards, aus 2 (Johan's statement, confirmed). Our lists: bounce malkavian 10 / 77 · tremere 8 · ventrue 6 · hacking 4 · nosferatu 0 · sewer 0. The odds are one hypergeometric line: P(>= 1 bounce in a 7-card hand) = 0.50 at 7 of 77 unseen, 0.37 once 4 are played (3 left in ~50 unseen), 0.57 at 10 of 90. CAVEATS: `_cats` has no 'wake' category and its 'stealth' count is loose (40 of 77 for malkavian) — the bank needs its own clean classes.
    - **64e. PROPOSED SHAPE (three tiers, each usable alone):** K0 DISCIPLINE PRIORS — from the disciplines printed on a seat's controlled vampires (public from turn 1): which card classes are possible at all and at what base rate. K1 DECK TEMPLATES — expected copies per class for a recognised archetype (our six lists now; TWD averages later), matched on clan + disciplines + cards seen. K2 ODDS — expected copies − copies seen played, over the seat's unseen cards (library + hand, both public counts), hypergeometric → P(class in hand). CONSUMERS, in order of value: the ACTOR's pump split (64b) · the clog's 'stealth bleeder' read (wakes at turn 3–4 today) · `spend-stealth`'s P(intercept) (today `_pCat`) · the bounce reserve's pump line (63a). The reactive statistics stay as the correction on top of the prior.
65. **THE KNOWLEDGE LAYER'S DATA — Johan uploaded `twd_decks.json` (21 Sep); read and tested for feasibility, nothing built.**
    - **65a. Johan's idea:** TWD + the starters (from vtes.json) as the base for assumptions. For every vampire and card an opponent plays, the accuracy of 'which archived deck does this resemble most' rises, and statistics and odds are weighted toward that — the bot updates its picture as cards are played. A clan can be played several ways (Malkavian as stealth + vote), so the STARTERS may be the ground until the played cards say more — the V5-era starters are fairly representative of how the decks tend to be played.
    - **65b. THE DATA (checked):** 4562 TWD decks, 1995–2026 (2019+: 1609), fields: cards {id: qty}, clan (1138 have none — multi-clan), traits, disciplines, capacity, creation_date, v5 shares. **Every card id resolves against our vtes.json (0 missing).** vtes.json yields **101 precon decks** from its `sets` entries (the Fifth Edition family alone: Hecata, Lasombra, Malkavian, Nosferatu, Toreador, Tremere, Ventrue + Anarch: Banu Haqim, Brujah, Gangrel, Ministry + Companion: Ravnos, Salubri, Tzimisce; 89 cards each). Johan's own example checks out: **91 % of Ventrue TWDs run Deflection, 6.4 copies on average** (2019+: 100 %, 6.6); Tremere 83 % / 5.5; Malkavian 40 % / 5.7 (two modes — his stealth + vote point); Nosferatu 15 %.
    - **65c. FEASIBILITY TEST (leave-one-out, 150 held-out 2019+ TWD decks, predict the deck's number of bounce cards; a deck-similarity posterior over all other decks, each observed card multiplying a deck's weight by its share of that card):** mean abs error **2.26 with the clan mean alone → 1.58 after TWO VAMPIRES → 1.29 after 3 more library cards → 1.15 after 6 → 1.10 after 10** (then flat). The crypt is the strongest early evidence; the posterior halves the error within the first few plays. Tested on tournament decks only — not on starters, not on casual decks.
    - **65c-GROUP (Johan, 21 Sep — tested the same day): THE CRYPT GROUP is a first-class key.** Johan: a vampire's group says a great deal about which deck is being played, above all for older decks and across the legacy → V5 shift — group 6 Malkavians no longer have Dementation as a clan discipline but Dominate. CHECKED on the 202 Malkavian TWDs by their highest crypt group: **G1–2: Dominate 19–35 library cards, Dementation 0 · G3–5: Dementation 6–20, Dominate 0–4 · G6–7: Dominate 19–31, Dementation 0** — three different decks under one clan name, split cleanly by group. As a predictor (398 held-out 2019+ decks, the number of bounce cards): **clan alone 2.12 → clan + the GROUP printed on ONE vampire 1.60** — as much as seeing two exact vampires (1.58), and available from the first vampire that comes out even when that exact vampire is rare in the archive. And the grouping rule is a HARD filter: 4559 of 4562 TWD crypts sit within two adjacent groups. → the knowledge file keys every reference deck on clan + group window; the posterior starts from that window, exact vampires and cards refine it.
    - **65c-MULTICLAN (Johan, 21 Sep — tested): in a multi-clan deck the DISCIPLINES are the archetype.** Johan: clan and discipline each carry value, and the discipline spread matters more the more mixed the clans become; but a multi-clan deck should be pinpointed quickly because it is usually a specific combination of vampires — often anarch decks, the newer Sabbat decks with Paths, and the traditional Obfuscate + Dominate decks mixing quality vampires on those two disciplines. TESTED on the 1138 TWD decks that carry no clan (200 held out, 2019+, predict the number of bounce cards): **global mean 3.56 → the discipline spread shared by the first two vampires 2.74 → posterior after 1 exact vampire 2.65 → after 2 vampires 1.75 → after 3 vampires 1.41**; the effective number of candidate decks falls **179 → 22 → 9** (of 4562). Both halves of his statement hold: the discipline spread is a real archetype key, and exact vampires pin a multi-clan deck down within two or three. Most common crypt discipline pairs among them: obf + pre 112, ani + pre 53, aus + obf 51, def + jud 49, aus + dom 47, ani + pot 46, dom + obf 45. CAVEAT: all 1138 carry the trait 'star' — 'no clan' in this file may mean 'star-vampire deck' rather than strictly multi-clan; check the field's meaning before building on it. → the knowledge file keys a reference deck on clan (when it has one) + group window + the crypt's discipline signature.
    - **65c-COVERAGE (Johan, 21 Sep — measured):** Johan: exact vampires will land Paths and anarch decks in the right TWD anyway; our own curated pool is small today (the first V5 starters + some of hacker / sewer); the plan is to curate the whole V5 library — legacy is enormous, but V5 curation tags hit many legacy cards too and give at least a rough idea of what a card does. MEASURED on the 1609 TWD decks from 2019+: **the LIBRARY is 74 % V5 on average (the crypt 39 %)** — his instinct holds for libraries; **81 % of all library copies played in those decks already have a cardfx entry with fx (auto tier), 14 % are hand-curated.** **CORRECTION (Johan caught it the same day):** my first list of 'most played cards with no fx' named Kine Resources Contested, Parity Shift, Information Highway and Ashur Tablets — all of which the bots PLAY. My script looked only at `modes`; effects also live in `ref` (referendum terms), `actGrant`, `handler`, `archetype`. The corrected figure and list are in the journal entry of 21 Sep ('RÄTTELSE: kort utan effekt'). **KEY SEPARATION: the knowledge layer needs a card's CLASS, not a playable fx.** A plain text classifier over vtes.json's 2364 library cards finds 12 bounce cards (cardfx's bounce category knows 5), 16 wakes, 64 combat ends, 138 intercept, 153 +bleed — rough (its 'stealth' 625 is far too wide; 'Dramatic Upheaval' is a false bounce) but it covers LEGACY at once. → classes come from text for the whole library and are overridden by curation where curation exists; what the bot can PLAY stays a curation job and grows with the V5 library.
    - **65d. PROPOSED USE:** (1) OFFLINE build step → one compact `elysium-knowledge.json`: per reference deck its clan, crypt ids, sparse card counts and CLEAN class counts (bounce, wake, intercept, stealth, bleed pump, combat ends, votes, rush); precons + TWD, with date. (2) PRIOR = the population the table is likely drawn from: a knob — starters weighted heavily at a casual / fill-a-seat table (Johan's ground), TWD for tournament-like play; recent decks weigh more. (3) UPDATE per observed vampire and played card (the 65c posterior; smoothing so one odd card never zeroes a deck). (4) PREDICT a mixture, not one 'most similar deck': P(class in hand) = Σ w_k × hypergeometric(unseen cards, copies_k − copies seen, hand size) — the mixture keeps Malkavian's two modes alive until a vote card or a stealth card settles it. (5) VALIDATE OFFLINE first (no matches needed): 65c's test per class, plus 'are our six bot decks recognised, and after how many cards?'. (6) Consumers as in 64e, the actor's pump split first.
    - **65e. Cautions:** TWD is a population of WINNING TOURNAMENT decks — casual decks look more like starters with upgrades (why the prior must be a knob). 1138 decks carry no clan. File size (10 MB raw) → the build step must slim it for the browser / hotseat.
    - **65f. Johan (17 Oct 2026) — the prey's WAKES as blockers belong here.** The probe lab (v0.6.206) lost on five tables of 91, all a prey with one unlocked body and a wake in hand: it blocked the prober with the body and woke for the punch. Counting a wake as a body is a knowledge-layer read in two parts: (a) does the deck hold wakes at all (the deck read — clan, the disciplines on its vampires, the cards it has shown, the TWD / starter references), and (b) how likely it is to hold one in hand NOW, given how many cards it has cycled and how many wakes it has played so far (the hypergeometric line of 64d / 65d). Then the probe's 'out-number' (`probeHold` 2, the default since v0.6.207) reads my actors against its unlocked bodies plus its expected wakes. Not built.
